1. Sudoku: rule out a number

In a 4 × 4 Sudoku, each row, column and 2 × 2 box contains 1, 2, 3 and 4 once. Here is a small part of a puzzle, rather than a complete board.

Question: A row reads 1 · blank · 3 · blank. The column through the first blank already contains a 4. Which number belongs in the first blank?

Show the answer and reasoning

The row is missing 2 and 4. The first blank cannot be 4 because its column already has a 4. Therefore it is 2, and the last blank in the row is 4. On a full board, also check the 2 × 2 box before placing a number.

When a learner gets stuck: Ask “Which numbers are missing?” before asking for an answer. Guessing a missing number without checking its column is the mistake this example targets. Next time, ask the learner to point to the clue that rules a number out.

2. Robot route: plan around an obstacle

Draw a 3 × 3 grid. Count rows from top to bottom and columns from left to right. Place the robot at row 1, column 1, the goal at row 3, column 3 and a rock at row 2, column 2. A move goes one square up, right, down or left. Diagonal moves are not allowed.

Question: Can the robot reach the goal in four moves without touching the rock? Write the arrows before tracing the route.

Show a route and explain its length

→ → ↓ ↓ travels along the top row and then down the right edge. It never enters the centre square. ↓ ↓ → → is another valid route. The goal is two columns right and two rows down from the start, so at least four moves are needed; these routes achieve that minimum.

When a learner gets stuck: Trace one arrow at a time with a finger. The sequence → ↓ → ↓ touches the rock on its second move. Reaching the correct final square is not enough: every intermediate square must also be valid. In the game, check its displayed board and move limit because they can differ from this example.

3. Water puzzle: record both containers

You have an empty 3-litre container A and an empty 5-litre container B. You may fill a container, empty it, or pour between them until the source is empty or the destination is full. There are no measuring marks. The goal is to leave exactly 4 litres in B.

One six-move solution
MoveA: 3 LB: 5 L
Start empty00
1. Fill B05
2. Pour B into A32
3. Empty A02
4. Pour B into A20
5. Fill B25
6. Pour B into A34

The last pour transfers only 1 litre because A already holds 2 of its 3 litres. B therefore keeps 5 − 1 = 4 litres. A common mistake is to subtract A’s full capacity on every pour. Subtract only its remaining space, or the available water if that is smaller.

Try independently: Starting empty again, obtain 2 litres in B. Use the table to check your work after trying.

Check the shorter challenge

Fill B, then pour B into A. A holds 3 litres and B holds 2 litres: two moves.

Turn a round into useful practice

  1. Before playing, name one goal: rule out a number, plan a route or track quantities.
  2. After a mistake, identify the first move that broke a rule. Change that move before restarting.
  3. After success, explain the solution without copying it. Try a fresh round only when ready.

A game score describes that attempt; it does not establish mastery of a school curriculum. If a timed round feels rushed, return to these untimed examples. For school-year practice, use the country and school-year selector and choose a topic already familiar from class.
